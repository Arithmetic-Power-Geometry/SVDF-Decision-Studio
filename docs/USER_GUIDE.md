# User Guide

## Page 1 — Context
State the organisation, decision owner, optional budget, decision date and the decision question.

Good: “Which sustainability investments deserve further funding review for FY 2027?”

Weak: “Which project is best?”

## Page 2 — Projects
Include alternatives that genuinely compete for the same decision attention.

## Page 3 — Shared value
Use consistent meanings across alternatives.

Economic value: annual savings, NPV, avoided cost, productivity gain or a declared score.

Environmental value: energy saved, CO2 avoided, water conserved, waste avoided or a declared score.

Stakeholder reach: people, customers, employees, students, communities, sites or service users materially benefiting.

Do not mix units inside one column.

## Page 4 — Complexity
Implementation burden: technical difficulty, approvals, procurement, specialist skill, disruption and coordination.

Scale: rollout size, number of sites, systems, departments, people or geographic reach.

Volatility: uncertainty or instability in the baseline/evidence that makes a project harder to judge or manage.

## Page 5 — Evidence
Record where every number came from.

High confidence: metered data, audited accounts, signed quotation, completed feasibility study.

Medium confidence: credible engineering estimate, internal analysis, pilot data.

Low confidence: early estimate, informal expert judgement, incomplete baseline.

## Page 6 — Preferences
The baseline IC weights are 0.40 burden, 0.35 scale and 0.25 volatility. They are modelling choices, not universal constants. Robustness varies both value and complexity weights.

## Page 7 — Frontier
A frontier project is not “the winner.” It is an option not unambiguously beaten on both lower complexity and higher shared value.

## Page 8 — Robustness
Frontier frequency tells you how often an option survives alternative weight specifications. It is not a probability of project success.

## Page 9 — Organisational answerability
Ask whether the institution can actually act on the evidence. A good project may still be blocked by missing authority, fragmented data, poor timing or lack of budget readiness.

## Page 10 — Report
Use the report as a decision record: inputs, assumptions, frontier, robustness, evidence quality, actionability and next steps.
